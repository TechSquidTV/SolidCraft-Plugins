All six plugins now share internal part definitions and verified component reuse.
Bearings and V-wheels add supplier products with visible exterior detail. The Bearing dialog also offers a supplier-neutral sealed 105.
CSK clutches add customizable exterior geometry with reference sources recorded
separately.

## Bearings and V-wheels

- **Bearings** offers base size and closure or a search by nominal bore, outside diameter and width. Thirteen closed variants use estimated exteriors. Twelve carry catalog-confirmed MISUMI numbers, and the sealed 105 has a supplier-neutral BOM row. Open remains available for 626. The [product table](../plugins/techsquid-bearings/README.md#supported-bearings-and-csk-presets) lists each exterior basis.
- **V-wheels** offers Solid, Xtreme Solid, grooved Delrin, Delrin Mini and Xtreme Mini products. Choose a bare wheel or a core comprising one wheel, two bearings and one internal shim.
- Direct bearings and wheel assemblies reuse the same verified bearing definition. The wheel package contains its dependencies; installing Bearings separately is optional.
- Core assemblies list their separate purchases in the BOM. They are not retail kits and do not include mounting axles, external spacers, washers or nuts.
- Geometry is built from native features using measured STEP profiles. Source hashes, dimensional discrepancies and the 105ZZ correction from 4.2 to 4 mm are documented. Original STEP files are not distributed.

Bearing closures are intentional zero-thickness surface bodies; rings are exterior
envelopes. These models support layout and appearance, not physical bearing mass,
internal contact or manufacturing sections. Metal wheels and the supplied inch and
8 × 16 × 5 bearings remain unpublished candidates pending exact product evidence.

## Bearings dialogs

The Bearings submenu has four commands. **Bearing** inserts a radial preset. **CSK clutch** inserts or edits a preset, or starts from scratch. It shows the inherited closure next to each starting preset. **Bearing by dimensions** and **CSK clutch by dimensions** share the numeric millimetre controls for nominal bore, outside diameter and width. See the [Bearings reference](../plugins/techsquid-bearings/REFERENCE.md).

## Customizable CSK clutches

- Reference publishers such as Formsprag are not assigned manufacturers. CSK specifications have no purchase identity or BOM part number. Purchasing assignment is [future work for a shared component workflow](../docs/parts-library.md#future-component-purchasing-workflow) after creation.
- Custom and unsourced parts remain countable in the BOM. Repeated identical geometry reuses a definition; different keyway values stay separate.
- Each component contains two chamfered solid rings and two cover/seal faces, with full-width rectangular keyways. Eight supplied STEP files establish measured exterior sections; other variants use explicitly recorded estimates. PP keyways default to opposing sides. Hidden sprags, raceways, seal cross-sections, rotation simulation and mass predictions remain outside the representation. Torque and load ratings are source-specific selection information, not geometry parameters.

See the [CSK example](../examples/bearings-and-clutches/#csk-references-and-overrides) and [source ledger](../docs/sources/csk.md).

## Nylon-insert lock nuts

The Hex nut dialog adds thin DIN 985 nylon-insert lock nuts. Choose the style and size to create a loose nut, a flush captive pocket, or both. The [V-slot gantry](../examples/v-slot-gantry/) uses four M5 lock nuts.

New custom nut descriptions include thread size, pitch, width across flats and height.

**BOM change for near-diameter inputs.** New insertions of `M5.0000001x0.8` and `M5` now share the nominal M5 part number. Existing saved definitions keep their original part numbers and descriptions. To consolidate a duplicate row in a saved design, replace its occurrences with the nominal size.

If Hex nuts is already installed, install the updated ZIP through **Preferences → Plugins** and review the replacement. SolidCraft ties plugin approvals to the exact package.

**Breaking change for installed-tool calls.** Scripts calling `techsquid-nuts.make-nut` must pass `style`. Add `"style": "Plain hex"` to keep creating plain nuts. Existing `plugin.run` recipes default to Plain hex.

## Extrusions and slot nuts

- **Slot nuts come in M3, M4 and M5.** Size picks the thread of the MISUMI pre-assembly (HNTT5) or post-assembly (HNTP5) nut. The outer shape stays the same within each type; the tapped hole and the part number change.
- **The Extrusion dialog starts with Mode**, then Slot, Size and Length. Holes from is now **Hole face** and Hole distances from is now **Measure holes from**; the keys `hole_face` and `holes_from` are unchanged.

## One parts-library foundation

Nut-pocket batches and repeated runs now reserve distinct names for their planes,
profiles, cuts and bolt holes. Existing names in other components, case variants
and numbered suffixes are respected, including after reopening a saved design.
Renaming a part and all its bodies no longer lets a replacement collide with the
original sketches. Face-attached pocket layouts preserve the selected copied or
nested assembly through consecutive insertions; hardware seat joints identify
that exact placement.

Packages expose insertion tools only. The shared internal descriptors retain
dimensions, provenance, attachment frames, configuration rules and assembly
members. Catalog discovery is deferred: the host currently displays every entry
as a command but cannot provide a useful Catalog form. No Catalog buttons or
public query handlers are shipped.

Product identity is separate from CAD detail, placement and display names.
Cosmetic/modeled threads can share a purchasing identity while retaining different
representations. Reuse verifies native features, sketches, body history, materials,
appearances, metadata and nested children. Edited definitions remain intact;
subsequent insertions create clean replacements. Component renaming and unrelated
parameter changes do not break reuse. Failed operations roll back as one host
transaction.

## Breaking changes and migration

- **Breaking Bearing dialog inputs.** Replace product values such as `{"size":"B6203ZZ"}` with `{"size":"6203","closure":"Shielded"}`. An empty direct plugin run now builds MISUMI B608ZZ. Pass `{"size":"625","closure":"Sealed"}` to retain the old default. Generated tool calls require Size and Closure. Refresh catalog selections for revision 11. Existing measured bearing definitions keep their identities and V-wheel members.

- **CSK scripts and identities change from earlier PR revisions.** Use `make-csk` for CSK designations; `make-bearing` accepts only the listed radial products. The former Formsprag product identity is replaced by a design specification with separate reference data. The former `part_number` and `purchase_*` workflow inputs are removed without aliases. CSK specification metadata is version 4 and its representation revision is 3. Refresh saved catalog resolutions. Old saved geometry remains, but insertion creates current definitions; no aliases or legacy identity fallback are provided.

- **Generated feature names change.** New section sketches and bearing features use the shared `Part: Feature` convention, keeping layout sketches outside the generated part’s naming prefix. Tapped-screw support axes use `Seat for Part: Axis …`, since they belong to the mounting part rather than the reusable screw. Scripts that address these new timeline features by name must use the new names. Existing saved geometry keeps its original names; no aliases or automatic migration are added.

- **The pre-release `catalog` API is removed.** Script calls to that entry are rejected. Use the documented insertion workflows directly; there is no compatibility alias or hidden handler.

- **Updated host inspection is required.** All six packages use `component.list.active_path` to preserve the selected copy and `sketch.inspect.world_plane` to orient parts on rotated sketches. SolidCraft builds from before merged host PR #17999 are insufficient; see [host requirements](../docs/parts-library.md#host-requirements). There is no guessed-context or local-plane fallback.
- **Slot nut BOM part numbers include the thread.** An M5 nut is now `MISUMI HNTT5-5` or `MISUMI HNTP5-5`.
- **Extrusion tool calls name `mode` and `length`.** Both are required fields now, so the dialog can show them first. An assistant or MCP client calling the Extrusion tool passes `"mode": "Fixed length"` or `"Sketch driven"`, and a `length`, which Sketch driven ignores. Scripts that run the plugin with `plugin.run` are unaffected: a field they leave out still takes its default.
- **Placement is a pick field.** Every dialog has one **Placement** field: pick sketch points or circles in the view, as in Extrude's Profiles, and points already selected fill it when the dialog opens. A click anywhere inside a circle picks it too and means its centre, so there is no need to hit the line; any other region is refused. Sketch-driven rails pick 1 to 16 straight lines from one sketch into it. The `sketch`, `sketch_point` and `path_lines` inputs are rejected without aliases; scripts pass `placement` as a list of references such as `{"type": "external_sketch_point", "sketch": "Holes", "id": "p1"}` `{"type": "external_sketch_curve", "sketch": 2, "id": "c1"}` or `{"type": "profile", "sketch": 2, "index": 0}`, the sketch by feature id or name. A named sketch no longer stands for all its lines. The dialog needs SolidCraft 0.44 or newer (host PR #18302); an older build leaves its Run off, and scripts still work.
- **Legacy definition metadata is not adopted.** Existing designs still open as native geometry, but the new library creates new verified definitions when inserting alongside pre-0.3 parts. There are no compatibility aliases or command-hash fallback paths.
- **Retired/unknown workflow fields are rejected.** For screws and nuts, replace `designation` with `size` and `custom_designation` with `custom_size`. For screws, replace `custom_head_diameter`/`custom_head_height` with `head_diameter`/`head_height`, and `hex`, `torx`, `slot` or `phillips` with `drive_size`. For nuts, replace `custom_height` with `custom_thickness`. For inserts, replace `modeled` with `thread_modeled`.
- **Unsupported modeled insert threads now fail explicitly.** Unified and pipe insert sizes require cosmetic threads. Scripts omitting `thread_modeled` use modeled geometry for metric sizes and cosmetic geometry for other sizes; the dialog exposes the choice.
- **Pre-release snapshot records change.** Version 2 verifies parameter dependencies through the host. Parts sealed with the earlier snapshot version are preserved; later insertions build current definitions. There is no compatibility fallback.
- **BOM numbers and classifications change.** Sourced supplier/standard configurations use their product numbers or standard designations. Explicit dimension overrides, screws with a drive their head's standard does not list (a Torx socket head is not an ISO 4762 screw), unsourced insert rows and finished cut/machined rails are custom models with generated model numbers. Numbers derive entirely from canonical products, so equivalent size spellings and different thread detail share a purchase row while different custom parts remain separate. Standard geometry does not imply supplier stock, grade, finish or certification.
- **Example paths change.** Update links and scripts that use `examples/<name>.json` or `examples/<name>.steps.json` to the corresponding folder paths in the [example gallery](../examples/). The bearings and CSK examples now share one design.
- Existing screw/hole and nut/pocket attachment workflows remain available. Sketch-driven rails remain independent path features. A definition that cannot be verified is preserved; a smaller batch may be required when inspection or construction reaches the command limit.

## Installation and examples

All six packages use version 0.3.0. Install the `techsquid-<plugin>-0.3.0.zip` files
through **Plugins > Install plugin…** using a build that meets the
[host requirements](../docs/parts-library.md#host-requirements). Each package
includes its guide, reference, shared internal-definition/reuse documentation and source ledger.

Every command now has its own icon in the Plugins menu and its dialog header on
SolidCraft 0.44 and later. The icons follow the light and dark themes.

Each example includes a saved design, captioned build steps and rendered previews.
Designs reopen without plugins installed. [Browse the example gallery](../examples/)

### Bearings dialog migration

**Breaking change for scripts and recipes.** Keep `make-bearing` for radial size and closure. Use `make-csk` for both catalog presets and custom CSK clutches. Update the other calls as follows.

| Old call | Replacement |
|---|---|
| `make-bearing` with `"size":"By dimensions"` | `make-bearing-by-dimensions`. Remove `size` and use `narrow_by_closure` for the former `closure` value. |
| `make-csk` with `"size":"By dimensions"` | `make-csk-by-dimensions`. Remove `size`, pass `match_bore`, `match_diameter` and `match_width` as numbers (for example, `15`), and rename `match_closure`, `match_inner_keyway` and `match_outer_keyway` to `narrow_by_closure`, `narrow_by_inner_keyway` and `narrow_by_outer_keyway`. |
| `make-custom-csk` | Use `make-csk` and replace `preset` with `size`. Keep the geometry override keys. |
| `make-csk` with `"size":"Custom"` | Use `"size":"From scratch"`. Keep the geometry override keys. |

The retired inputs are rejected. Existing designs keep their geometry and component identities.

## Validation

Validated with an unsigned development host containing the required inspection fields: native source geometry and
wheel/rail contact, internal catalog descriptors, insertion-only package entries,
all 55 CSK presets, measured ring profiles, custom dimensions/keyway angles, and separate reference records,
shared definitions and BOM quantities,
modified-part detection, nested placement, undo/redo, forced late rollback,
save/reopen and command budgets. Existing geometry, rail machining, screw/nut
attachments, path-driven rails and saved examples are regression checked alongside
Rust unit tests, formatting, native/Wasm Clippy and package/schema drift checks.
